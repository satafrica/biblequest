# Illustration guide: how people are depicted

Owner's rule (22 Nov 2026): people in Bible scenes should show a realistic range of skin tones, and anyone
the Bible describes as Ethiopian (Cushite) must be clearly shown with dark skin.

## People the KJV describes as Ethiopian (show with dark skin)
- Moses' wife, "an Ethiopian woman" (Numbers 12:1). Often identified with Zipporah; the app's Who-am-I card covers Zipporah.
- Ebedmelech the Ethiopian, who rescued Jeremiah (Jeremiah 38:7).
- The man of Ethiopia, treasurer of Candace, baptised by Philip (Acts 8:27).
- Jeremiah 13:23 ("Can the Ethiopian change his skin") shows Ethiopians were known for dark skin.

## Probably dark-skinned, but the text doesn't say (use a range of brown skin tones; don't claim certainty in notes)
- Simeon "that was called Niger" (Acts 13:1); Niger is Latin for "black".
- Lucius of Cyrene (Acts 13:1) and Simon a Cyrenian (Mark 15:21), both from North Africa.

## Everyone else
- Israelites, Galileans (including Jesus), Egyptians, Persians and other peoples of the region: olive to brown skin, varied within each crowd.
- Interpretation notes must say the appearance is imagined, citing a verse when one supports the variety.
